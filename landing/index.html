Everything you need. Nothing you have to trust us with.

Zero-knowledge by design

The master password is never sent to the server. Even a full database dump cannot unlock a vault.

Passkeys as a second factor

Keep your master password and add a hardware-backed passkey. A one-time recovery code is the fallback.

Encrypted backups

Export a password-protected copy of the vault and merge it back later without exposing plaintext.

Recipient-bound sharing

Share an item with another Rook account. The key is wrapped to their public key, so only they can open it.

History and conflict recovery

Every edit is versioned. If two tabs diverge, merge both sides without discarding either encrypted state.

Audit without an audience

Strength, reuse, and age checks run locally. Your vault is never uploaded for analysis.

The server is a dumb encrypted-blob store

Your master password never leaves your device.

Rook derives two independent keys in your browser: one proves who you are, the other decrypts your vault. The server stores only a hash of the auth proof and ciphertext.

  • Argon2id with a 64 MiB memory cost
  • Separate auth and vault keys via HKDF-SHA256
  • Client-side XChaCha20-Poly1305 vault encryption
  • Recipient-bound shares with ephemeral ECDH
